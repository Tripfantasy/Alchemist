# Source Quality & Confidence

Reference for the research-report workflow. Keeps "credible" from being a matter of vibe.

## Source tiers

Prefer higher tiers. Mixing tiers is fine — labeling them is mandatory.

| Tier | Source type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Systematic review, meta-analysis, consensus statement | Best evidence when recent and on-point |
| 2 | Peer-reviewed primary research | The workhorse. Check sample size and whether it replicated |
| 3 | Preprint (bioRxiv, medRxiv, arXiv) | Legitimate and often current — always label as unreviewed |
| 4 | Authoritative body or official documentation | NIH, FDA, WHO, standards bodies, a tool's own docs |
| 5 | Reputable technical press, conference talks, theses | Good for orientation and leads; weak as sole support |
| 6 | Vendor material, company blogs, marketing | Last resort. Always labeled. Never the only support for a claim |

**Tier is not the whole story.** A 2019 Tier-2 paper superseded by a 2025 result outranks nothing. Recency, direct relevance, and independence all matter alongside tier.

## Confidence rubric

Assign to every load-bearing claim.

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **High** | Multiple independent peer-reviewed sources agree; a genuine search for contradiction found none |
| **Moderate** | 2+ sources agree, but replication is limited — or the support mixes peer-reviewed and preprint |
| **Low** | Single source, preprint-only, or credible disagreement exists |

**Independent** means separate evidence. Three news articles covering one study are **one** source. Three papers from the same group are closer to one than three.

## Red flags — note inline when present

- n-of-1, tiny samples, or no control group
- Undisclosed or significant conflicts of interest
- Press release cited where the underlying paper exists — always go to the paper
- Findings older than the field's half-life (weeks for LLM tooling, years for structural biology)
- A claim that traces back to a single origin no matter how many places repeat it
- Retracted or heavily-corrected papers — worth a check on anything load-bearing
- Preprints more than ~2 years old that never reached peer review

## Citation discipline

- Never cite a URL you didn't fetch.
- Abstract-only reads get marked as such.
- Record the publication date for everything.
- If a source doesn't actually say what you need, drop it — don't stretch it.
